# Q-U reply — does SSL continuation from a strong init beat the same checkpoint used directly?

Short answer: **yes, it can pay, but the gains are modest, scale-dependent, and distribution-sensitive. There is no knee-MRI-specific measurement; the cleanest evidence is chest X-ray and CT. The honest negative is as important as the positive.**

## 1. Measured deltas with the continuation control present

I found three relevant settings where the same strong checkpoint is evaluated with and without in-domain SSL continuation.

### A. Chest X-ray (CXR) — controlled scale and compute

[Data Scaling Laws for Radiology Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12818) (Ilse et al., 2025) is the cleanest study. They take two open-weights encoders — **MI2** (CLIP-style, trained on ~500k–3.8M medical image-text pairs) and **RAD-DINO** (DINOv2-style, ~840k CXRs) — and continually pretrain them on nested subsets (30k, 50k, 100k, 1M, 3.5M) of a single-institution CXR corpus. Downstream evaluation uses the same classifier/segm head, same compute budget.

| model | in-domain finding classification | lines-and-tubes segmentation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MI2 open-weights | baseline | baseline |
| MI2 + 30k CXR SSL | **surpasses open-weights** | — |
| MI2 + 3.5M CXR SSL | +6% AUPRC on rib fracture vs. OWM; beats OWM on all 20 finding tasks | — |
| RAD-DINO open-weights | baseline | baseline |
| RAD-DINO + 100k CXR SSL | **surpasses open-weights** | surpasses open-weights at 100k |
| RAD-DINO + 3.5M CXR SSL | beats OWM | significantly outperforms all others |

Key point: the delta exists, but the absolute gains are class-dependent and the average AUPRC improvements are described as "modest." The strongest single-class move is +6% AUPRC.

### B. CT segmentation — DINOv3 continued on 3.87M CT slices

[MedDINOv3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02379) (Li et al., 2025) initializes a DINOv3 ViT-B from LVD-1689M and performs domain-adaptive SSL on CT-3M (3.87M axial CT slices). They hold architecture and fine-tuning protocol constant.

| AMOS22 abdominal-organ segmentation (DSC) | setting |
| --- | --- |
| 85.51% | DINOv3 init, no further SSL on CT-3M |
| 86.58% | + Stage 1 DINOv2-style self-distillation on CT-3M (+1.07%) |
| 86.54% | + gram anchoring (no gain) |
| 87.38% | + high-resolution adaptation (+0.84%, total +1.87% over no CT SSL) |

So on CT, continuing DINOv3 with a DINO-family objective yields about **+1.9 percentage points DSC** with ~4M in-domain unlabeled slices.

### C. Natural / general-medical image segmentation with adapters

[GLARE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17816) (Chhatkuli et al., 2025) initializes a DINO or UDI backbone, freezes it, and trains only lightweight adapters during continual SSL. On semantic segmentation:

| dataset | UDI baseline | UDI → GLARE |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ADE20k | baseline | +0.4 mIoU |
| LoveDA (satellite, out-of-domain) | baseline | +0.6 mIoU |

On classification (Derm7pt dermoscopy and COVIDx X-ray), DINO → GLARE consistently outperforms DINO alone. The deltas are small and the task is dense prediction, but the control is present.

## 2. Corpus size at which it starts paying

The literature gives a clear threshold picture:

* **30k in-domain samples** are enough for the CLIP-style MI2 to surpass its open-weights checkpoint on in-domain CXR classification.
* **100k in-domain samples** are needed for the DINOv2-style RAD-DINO to surpass its open-weights checkpoint on the same CXR tasks.
* **~1M–3.5M samples** are where the gains become reliable across classes and segmentation tasks.
* **3.87M CT slices** buy +1.07% DSC in MedDINOv3 stage 1.

Against this, **fastMRI singlecoil train is roughly ~1,000 knee exams** at the size we are pulling. That is two orders of magnitude below the 30k–100k threshold where the CXR studies first see a benefit. Unless the corpus is much larger than the singlecoil split, the prior that continuation beats the DINOv2/DINOv3 init is weak.

## 3. Whether the objective has to match

All the positive evidence is **objective-matched**:

* RAD-DINO is DINOv2-style and is continued with DINOv2 losses.
* MI2 is CLIP/UniCL and is continued with UniCL.
* MedDINOv3 explicitly uses a "DINOv2-style self-distillation" stage on CT-3M to continue DINOv3.
* GLARE uses a DINO-like global consistency loss.

I found **no study** that takes a DINOv2/DINOv3 checkpoint and continues it under a *different* SSL objective (MAE, SimCLR, BYOL) with a measured downstream gain. The authors of MedDINOv3 chose DINO-family objectives precisely because mixing objectives is not obviously continuation. So the honest answer is: the evidence is all objective-matched, and a cross-objective continuation is an untested transfer.

## 4. The nearest honest negatives

These are more important than the positives for pricing fastMRI.

* **Out-of-domain CXR (VinDR):** In the Data Scaling Laws paper, RAD-DINO continual pretraining on INST-CXR-BENCH shows a **reversed scaling trend** on the external VinDR benchmark — more in-domain SSL makes it worse than the open-weights checkpoint. The authors attribute this to distribution shift and catastrophic forgetting. MI2 also needs **substantially more than 100k samples** to beat open-weights on VinDR.
* **Small / out-of-domain target data in GLARE:** The authors note that continual pretraining on a small out-of-domain set can be detrimental; ADE20k (closer to ImageNet) does not show this, but LoveDA can.
* **ImageNet vs. RadImageNet:** [Revisiting Hidden Representations in Transfer Learning for Medical Imaging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08272) (Juodelyte et al., 2023) found that ImageNet-pretrained ResNet50 often outperforms RadImageNet-pretrained weights on seven medical classification tasks after fine-tuning. This is supervised pretraining, not SSL, but it reinforces that medical-domain pretraining is not automatic — domain similarity matters.

## Honest null for knee MRI

I found **no published measurement** of continuing DINOv2/DINOv3/MAE on an unlabeled knee-MRI corpus and comparing it to the same checkpoint fine-tuned directly. The nearest adjacent positives are:

* CXR: +6% AUPRC on a single class, modest average gains, reliable only at ≥100k–3.5M images.
* CT: +1.07% DSC from ~4M slices of DINO-family continuation.

The nearest honest negative is the same CXR paper's external benchmark, where continuation underperformed the strong init. Given that fastMRI is likely <<30k studies and the target distribution may not be perfectly matched, the expected value of a continuation arm is **small and unproven**, not a guaranteed route to 0.952.

## What this means for us

If we still want to price the fastMRI lane, the experiment that would answer it is not "does SSL on knee MRI help?" but "does DINOv2 + DINO-family continuation on our actual fastMRI subset beat DINOv2 used directly on our target task, on our holdout?" The literature says that experiment is worth running only if the unlabeled corpus is at least in the tens-of-thousands-of-exams range and the objective stays in the DINO family. At ~1,000 exams, the honest prior is that it does not move the metric.
