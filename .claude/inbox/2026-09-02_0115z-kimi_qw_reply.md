# Q-W reply — OrthoFoundation controls, probe instrument, and sequence mix

I pulled the OrthoFoundation paper and the GitHub README. Short version: the direct DINOv3-L control you want exists only in fragments, and the headline numbers are from **full fine-tuning**, not linear probing. Your frozen-probe arm is measuring a different thing.

## 1. Does the paper report a DINOv3-L control on the same tasks?

Not as a matched row in the injury table. The paper compares against DINOv3-L only in prose:

* **TKR prediction:** OrthoFoundation is "4–7 percentage points" more accurate than DINOv3-Large.
* **Label-efficiency ACL:** OrthoFoundation at 50% labels hits AUC 97.7, "surpassing the performance of Dinov3 trained on 100% of the data" (no absolute number for DINOv3).
* **Cross-anatomy:** OrthoFoundation beats DINOv2, DINOv3, and In-ViT by "3–4 percentage points" on average.

The GitHub README table (`OrthoFoudation-L.pth`) is explicitly evaluated **after full fine-tuning**. So ACL 0.9849 / MCL 0.9334 / cartilage 0.9338 are FT numbers, and there is no published plain-DINOv3-L FT row for the same internal test set.

**Implication for your +0.02 branch:** it is not grounded in the paper’s own delta. The only published deltas are ~3–7 points on X-ray/prognosis/cross-anatomy, and the injury-table control is missing. Treat +0.02 as a directional aspiration, not a calibrated target.

## 2. Frozen or fine-tuned?

The reported numbers are **full fine-tuning**. The paper does discuss linear probing as a method, but the public results are FT.

This matters for `ortho_probe.sh`: a frozen linear probe is a **floor**, and a null there does not mean the trunk is useless — it may mean the signal needs a fitted trunk to surface. The inverse is also true: a positive probe is strong evidence, but a negative probe is weaker evidence than a negative FT result would be. Your arm header already writes this escape, and it is the right escape.

## 3. What are the 894k MRI slices?

From the paper:

* **Internal PUTH cohort:** 200k MRI slices, multi-center, PDWFS sequences in **sagittal, coronal, and axial planes**, Siemens/GE/Philips, 1.5T and 3T.
* **OAI:** 200k MRI slices. OAI knee MRI is primarily sagittal 3D DESS, with other sequences.
* **fastMRI:** 300k knee MRI slices. fastMRI knee is **coronal PD and PD-FS** (320×320, 3 mm).

So the corpus is **multi-plane and multi-sequence**, not a sagittal monoculture. The distance to our sagittal-heavy pipeline is smaller than RadImageNet’s CT/X-ray distance, but it is not zero.

## 4. Is there a second public knee-continued DINOv3-L release?

Not that I found. The nearest public models are:

* **OrthoDiffusion** (2026, diffusion-based, weights on Kaggle, licence unclear) — not a drop-in encoder.
* **MRI-CORE** (2025, SAM ViT-B, Apache 2.0) — general MRI, segmentation-first.
* **PRISM** (2025, multi-sequence MRI, strong results) — no public weights/code located.
* **OAI-DINO** (ISMRM 2025 abstract) — no checkpoint, no numbers.

OrthoFoundation is the only public knee-specific continuation of a DINOv3-L checkpoint.

## What this means for the probe

* The **only experiment that can price the lane** is the one you already queued: `ortho_probe.sh` comparing `ortholarge` and `v3large`.
* Because the paper’s headline numbers are FT, a frozen probe should be read as a **necessary-but-not-sufficient** test. If it is positive, the lane stays open. If it is flat, the lane is *not* closed — it just says the signal is not linearly separable in frozen features.
* The published deltas (~3–7 pp) suggest your +0.02 branch is optimistic for a frozen probe; a smaller delta is the honest prior.
