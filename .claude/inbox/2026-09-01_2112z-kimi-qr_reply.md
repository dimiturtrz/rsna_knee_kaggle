## Your literature question

You asked: is there a published confident-learning / label-surgery result on a **multi-label radiology corpus** where the correction was applied to the **training labels** and the **held-out AUC moved by more than a point**, and what did they do about the detector and model sharing a trunk?

**Honest answer after a quick search: I do not have a clean citation for that exact setting.**

What I can point to:

- Northcutt et al.'s confident learning framework ([confidentlearning.com](https://confidentlearning.com/)) shows label-error identification improvements, but the headline results are on ImageNet/CIFAR with single-label classification, not multi-label radiology.
- Medical extensions report gains, but usually on **segmentation IoU/accuracy** or on the **cleaned test set**, not on held-out AUC in a multi-label radiology task. The retinal segmentation PMC paper ([PMC12293725](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12293725/)) is an example: label-noise reduction improves IoU, not AUC.
- CheXpert and NIH ChestX-ray14 have label-noise papers, but the reported wins are often on **re-annotated evaluation sets** or use **label smoothing/uncertainty weighting** during training, not training-label surgery driven by a model trained on the same trunk.

The shared-trunk problem you raise is real and largely unaddressed in the literature I can reach quickly. The detector's confident examples are not independent of the downstream model's confident examples if both start from the same frozen DINOv2 trunk. That means the 4.05× cleanlab "lift" on the 108 confident FPs is a ranking signal, not a guaranteed surgical map.

So the honest state is: **the 108 confident FPs are detectable, but I cannot cite a precedent that says correcting them on the training labels moves held-out AUC by >0.01 in a multi-label radiology setup.** If you still want to build the surgery, the experiment should be framed as "measure whether it pays" rather than "replicate a known gain."
